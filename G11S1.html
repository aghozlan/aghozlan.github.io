Exercise 1: The Parabolic Path
A particle \( M \) moves in a Cartesian coordinate system \( (O; \vec{i}, \vec{j}) \). Its position vector at any time \( t \geq 0 \) is given by:

\( \vec{r}(t) = (2t)\vec{i} + (-5t^2 + 10t)\vec{j} \)

(Units: \( \vec{r} \) in meters, \( t \) in seconds)
1.1 Extract the parametric equations of motion \( x(t) \) and \( y(t) \).
1.2 Determine the equation of the trajectory \( y(x) \). What is the shape of the path?
1.3 At what instant(s) \( t \) does the particle pass through the x-axis?
Exercise 2: Displacement & Specific Positions
A second particle moves such that its position vector is defined by:

\( \vec{r}(t) = (4t)\vec{i} + (t^2 - 1)\vec{j} \)

(Units: \( \vec{r} \) in meters, \( t \) in seconds)
2.1 Calculate the position vectors of the particle at \( t_1 = 0 \text{ s} \) and \( t_2 = 2 \text{ s} \).
2.2 Calculate the displacement vector \( \Delta\vec{r} \) between \( t_1 \) and \( t_2 \), then find its magnitude.
2.3 Determine the instant \( t \) when the particle reaches an ordinate of \( y = 3 \text{ m} \).
Exercise 3: Linear Kinematics in 2D
The position vector of a third particle is given by the relation:

\( \vec{r}(t) = (3t + 2)\vec{i} + (6t - 4)\vec{j} \)

(Units: \( \vec{r} \) in meters, \( t \) in seconds)
3.1 Write the parametric equations \( x(t) \) and \( y(t) \). What is the initial position \( (t=0) \)?
3.2 Find the equation of the trajectory \( y(x) \). Deduce the geometric nature of the path.
3.3 Find the instant when the abscissa is \( x = 8 \text{ m} \), and deduce the ordinate at this instant.
