That salt dissolves in your mouth into tiny charged particles called \textbf{ions}, helping your nervous system send electrical signals! \end{tcolorbox} \section*{Part 1: The Anatomy of an Atom} Every atom consists of a central dense \textbf{nucleus} and a vast \textbf{electron cloud}. \begin{table}[h] \centering \begin{tabular}{@{}llccc@{}} \toprule \textbf{Particle} & \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Location} & \textbf{Relative Mass} & \textbf{Relative Charge} \\ \midrule Proton & $p^+$ & Nucleus & 1 amu & 1+ \\ Neutron & $n^0$ & Nucleus & 1 amu & 0 \\ Electron & $e^-$ & Electron Cloud & Negligible ($\sim$0) & 1- \\ \bottomrule \end{tabular} \end{table} \noindent\textbf{Important Charge Rules for $^A_Z X$:} \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{Charge of nucleus:} $q_{\text{nucleus}} = Z \times 1+ = +Z$ \item \textbf{Charge of electron cloud:} $q_{\text{cloud}} = \text{Number of } e^- \times 1-$ \item \textbf{Total charge (Neutral Atom):} $q_{\text{total}} = (+Z) + (-Z) = 0$ \end{itemize} \section*{Part 2: The Charge Game (Neutral vs. Ions)} Atoms gain or lose electrons to become stable, forming charged particles called \textbf{Ions}. \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{CATIONS (+):} Atoms that \textbf{lose} electrons (e.g., metals). Example: Na loses 1 $e^-$ $\rightarrow$ Na$^+$. \item \textbf{ANIONS (-):} Atoms that \textbf{gain} electrons (e.g., non-metals). Example: F gains 1 $e^-$ $\rightarrow$ F$^-$. \end{itemize} \section*{Part 3: Isotopes - The Atom's Twins} \textbf{Isotopes} are atoms of the \textit{same element} with the \textbf{same atomic number $Z$} but a \textbf{different mass number $A$} (different number of neutrons). \begin{table}[h] \centering \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}} \toprule \textbf{Isotope} & \textbf{Protons ($Z$)} & \textbf{Neutrons ($A-Z$)} & \textbf{Abundance} \\ \midrule Chlorine-35 ($^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}$) & 17 & 18 & 75\% (Highest Abundance) \\ Chlorine-37 ($^{37}_{17}\text{Cl}$) & 17 & 20 & 25\% (Heaviest Atom) \\ \bottomrule \end{tabular} \end{table} \noindent \textbf{Average Mass Formula:} Average A = $\frac{(A_1 \times \%_1) + (A_2 \times \%_2)}{100} = \frac{(35 \times 75) + (37 \times 25)}{100} = 35.5$ \vspace{0.5cm} \hrule \section*{Brevet-Style Challenge} \textit{Apply your knowledge to solve the following official exam-style questions.
