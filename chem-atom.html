Part 1: The Anatomy of an Atom

Particle Location Mass Charge
Proton (p+) Nucleus 1 amu 1+
Neutron (n0) Nucleus 1 amu 0
Electron (e-) Cloud ~0 1-
Representation: AZX
• Z = Atomic Number (Protons)
• A = Mass Number (Protons + Neutrons)
Charge Rules:
• qnucleus = Z × 1+
• qcloud = Number of e- × 1-
• Neutral Atom: qtotal = 0

Part 2: Neutral Atoms vs. Ions

Atoms gain or lose electrons to become stable (like noble gases), becoming charged Ions.

CATIONS (+): Atoms that lose electrons (usually metals). Losing negatives makes them positive.
Ex: Na loses 1 e- → Na+
ANIONS (-): Atoms that gain electrons (usually non-metals). Gaining negatives makes them negative.
Ex: F gains 1 e- → F-

Part 3: Isotopes (The Atom's Twins)

Definition: Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same atomic number Z but a different mass number A (different number of neutrons).
Example: Chlorine-35 (75% abundance) and Chlorine-37 (25% abundance).
Average Mass Formula: Average A = [ (A1 × %1) + (A2 × %2) ] / 100

Solve the following official exam-style questions. Show all justifications.

1. Aluminum (Al): Z = 13, A = 27.
Determine the composition of the neutral atom.
2. Calculate the relative charge of the nucleus of the Aluminum atom.
3. Aluminum loses 3 electrons to form an ion.
a) Is it a cation or an anion? Justify.
b) Write the symbol of the Aluminum ion.
c) Calculate the total relative charge of this ion.
4. Copper isotopes: 6329Cu (69%) & 6529Cu (31%).
a) Justify that these are isotopes.
b) Which is the heaviest? Explain.
c) Calculate the average mass number.
5. Neutral atom X: relative charge of electron cloud is 16-.
a) Deduce the atomic number Z.
b) If A = 32, find the number of neutrons.
6. Magnesium (Z = 12) forms the Mg2+ ion.
a) Is this a cation or anion? Justify.
b) Determine the number of electrons in this ion.
7. Neutral atom Y has 15 protons and 16 neutrons.
a) Write its representation AZY.
b) Prove the total charge is zero.
8. "The mass of an atom is almost entirely concentrated in its electron cloud."
True or False? Correct it if false.

Official Brevet Point Distribution Guide — Sets 1 & 2

Set 1 Answers

1. Al atom composition: 1.5 pts
• Protons = Z = 13 (0.5)
• Electrons = Protons = 13 (0.5)
• Neutrons = A - Z = 27 - 13 = 14 (0.5)
2. Charge of nucleus: 1.0 pt
qnucleus = Z × 1+ = 13 × 1+ = 13+ (1.0)
3. Aluminum Ion: 2.0 pts
a) Cation (0.5); formed by loss of electrons (0.5).
b) Symbol: Al3+ (0.5)
c) Total charge = 3+ (0.5)
4. Copper Isotopes: 3.5 pts
a) Same element, same Z (29), different A (63, 65) (1.0).
b) 6529Cu is heaviest; greater mass number (more neutrons) (1.0).
c) Average A = [(63 × 69) + (65 × 31)] / 100 (0.5)
= [4347 + 2015] / 100 = 63.62 (1.0)

Set 2 Answers

5. Unknown Atom X: 2.0 pts
a) qcloud = -16, electrons = 16. Neutral atom: protons (Z) = electrons. Z = 16 (1.0)
b) Neutrons = A - Z = 32 - 16 = 16 (1.0)
6. Magnesium Ion: 2.0 pts
a) Cation (0.5); positive charge indicates it lost 2 electrons (0.5).
b) Neutral Mg (12 e-) lost 2 = 10 e- (1.0)
7. Atom Y: 2.5 pts
a) A = 15 + 16 = 31. Representation: 3115Y (1.5)
b) qnucleus = 15+, qcloud = 15-.
qtotal = (+15) + (-15) = 0 (1.0)
8. True/False: 1.5 pts
False. (0.5)
Correction: Mass is almost entirely concentrated in the nucleus. (1.0)
