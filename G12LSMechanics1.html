PHYSICS

Chapter XI: WORK AND ENERGY

Use: $g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$
I. Lesson Summary

1. Work of a Constant Force

The work done by a constant force $\vec{F}$ moving an object from A to B is: $W_{AB}(\vec{F}) = \vec{F} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = F \cdot AB \cdot \cos(\alpha)$ Where $\alpha$ is the angle between $\vec{F}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
• $W > 0$: Motive Work
• $W < 0$: Resistive Work (e.g., friction)
• $W = 0$: Null Work (Force $\perp$ Displacement)

2. Kinetic Energy (K.E)

The energy an object possesses due to its motion. $K.E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ • $m$: mass in kg
• $v$: speed in m/s
• $K.E$: Kinetic Energy in Joules (J)

3. Work-Energy Theorem

The variation in kinetic energy of a particle between two points A and B is equal to the algebraic sum of the work done by all external forces applied to it. $\Delta K.E = K.E_B - K.E_A = \sum W_{AB}(\vec{F}_{ext})$

4. Gravitational Potential Energy ($P.E_g$)

The energy a system possesses due to its altitude relative to a chosen reference level ($P.E_g = 0$). $P.E_g = mgz$ • $z$: algebraic altitude from reference level (m)

5. Work Done by Weight

The work done by gravity depends only on the initial and final height, independent of the path taken. $W = mg(h_1 - h_2)$ • $h_1$: Initial height (m)
• $h_2$: Final height (m)
Note: If $h_1 > h_2$ (falling), $W > 0$. If $h_1 < h_2$ (rising), $W < 0$.

6. Elastic Potential Energy ($P.E_e$)

The energy stored in compressible or stretchable objects (like a spring or rubber band). $P.E_e = \frac{1}{2} k x^2$ • $k$: Spring constant (N/m), representing stiffness.
• $x$: Displacement from the equilibrium position (m).
Note: Since $x$ is squared, $P.E_e$ is always positive.

7. Mechanical Energy (M.E)

The sum of an object's kinetic and potential energies. $M.E = K.E + P.E_g + P.E_e$

8. Conservation of M.E

• Conserved: If only conservative forces (e.g., weight) do work. $\Delta M.E = 0 \Rightarrow M.E_A = M.E_B$.
• Non-Conserved: If friction or drag acts, they vary M.E. $\Delta M.E = \sum W_{nc}$
II. Exercises
Exercise 1
During a volcanic eruption, a volcano throws a rock (R) considered as a particle, of mass $m = 20 \text{ kg}$ from a point O, $36 \text{ m}$ above the surface of the ground. The rock is thrown with an initial velocity $\vec{V}_0$ making an angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal and having a magnitude of $V_0 = 24 \text{ m/s}$.

S is the highest point reached by the rock, and it is at an altitude of $H = 3.4 \text{ m}$ above point O. Take the horizontal plane containing O as a reference level for gravitational potential energy ($P.E_g = 0$) and neglect air resistance.
  1. The mechanical energy of the system [(R) - Earth] is conserved. Why? Calculate its value.
    1. Calculate the gravitational potential energy of the system [(R) - Earth] at point S.
    2. Apply the principle of the conservation of mechanical energy to determine the speed of the rock $V_S$ at the highest point S.
    3. Knowing that $V_S = V_0 \cos(\alpha)$, deduce the value of $\alpha$.
  3. Apply the work-energy theorem to determine the speed $V_A$ of the rock as it reaches the ground.

In this problem, we study the ascending motion of the object.

Exercise 3
Consider a skier taken as a particle moving along a path ABCD in the vertical plane. The mass of the skier and his equipment is $M = 60 \text{ kg}$. Along the track BC, whose length is $4 \text{ m}$, the skier is acted upon by a constant friction force of magnitude $f = 60 \text{ N}$. The tracks AB and CD are frictionless.

The horizontal plane containing BC is taken as a reference level for gravitational potential energy.

The skier starts from rest from point A at an altitude $h_A = 3.5 \text{ m}$ above the track BC.
  1. Along the track AB
    1. Determine the mechanical energy of the system Skier-Earth when the skier is at point A.
    2. The skier reaches point B with a speed $V_B = \sqrt{70} \text{ m/s}$. Prove that the track AB is frictionless.
  2. Along the track BC
    1. Determine the work done by each of the external forces acting on the skier between B and C.
    2. Deduce the speed of the skier at C.
    3. The kinetic energy lost between B and C is converted into another form of energy. Name it.
  3. Along the track CD
    1. Calculate:
      1. the mechanical energy of the system Skier-Earth when the skier is at point C.
      2. the gravitational potential energy of the system Skier-Earth when the skier is at point D.
    2. Deduce that the skier cannot reach point D.
    3. In reality, I is the highest point the skier can reach before returning back. Determine the height $h_I$ of point I above the track BC.
Exercise 4
An object (S), taken as a particle of mass $m = 2 \text{ kg}$, is released from rest at a point A of the track ABC.
Given: $\sin(\alpha) = 0.2$; $\cos(\alpha) = 0.98$; $AB = 4 \text{ m}$ and BC is frictionless.
The horizontal plane containing BC is taken as a reference level for gravitational potential energy.
    1. (S) reaches a point B with a speed $V_B = 3 \text{ m/s}$. Determine the magnitude of the constant friction force exerted by the track AB on (S).
    2. Determine the instantaneous power of: the friction force, normal force, and the weight of (S), when the speed of (S) becomes $2 \text{ m/s}$.
    3. It takes the object $2.7 \text{ s}$ to move from A to B. Deduce the average power of the friction force acting on (S) between A and B.
  1. The object (S) continues its motion and reaches a point C, where it collides with another object (P) of mass $M = 3 \text{ kg}$, attached to one end of an inextensible light vertical cord of length $l = 50 \text{ cm}$. The other end of the cord is attached to a fixed point O.
    Right after collision between (S) and (P), object (P) is ejected with a speed of $V_0 = 1.6 \text{ m/s}$. When (P) reaches point D, the cord is deviated by a maximum angle $\beta$. Determine the value of $\beta$.
  2. As object (P) reaches point D, the cord is cut and (P) falls vertically downwards.
    1. Determine the mechanical energy of the system [(P) - Earth] at point D.
    2. Determine the speed of (P) and its altitude above ground when its kinetic energy becomes equal to the gravitational potential energy of the system [(P) - Earth].
    3. Determine the speed $V_{ground}$ of (P) upon reaching the ground.

Exercise 1
  1. The only force doing work is the weight (conservative force), as air resistance is neglected.
    $M.E_O = K.E_O + P.E_O = \frac{1}{2}(20)(24^2) + 20(10)(36) = 5760 + 7200 = $ 12960 J.
    1. $z_S = 36 + 3.4 = 39.4 \text{ m}$. $P.E_S = mgz_S = 20(10)(39.4) = $ 7880 J.
    2. Conservation of M.E: $M.E_S = M.E_O \Rightarrow K.E_S + P.E_S = 12960 \Rightarrow \frac{1}{2}(20)V_S^2 + 7880 = 12960 \Rightarrow 10 V_S^2 = 5080 \Rightarrow V_S = \sqrt{508} \approx$ 22.54 m/s.
    3. $\cos(\alpha) = \frac{V_S}{V_0} = \frac{22.54}{24} = 0.939 \Rightarrow \alpha = \cos^{-1}(0.939) \approx$ 20.1°.
  3. $\Delta K.E = \sum W \Rightarrow \frac{1}{2} m V_A^2 - \frac{1}{2} m V_0^2 = W(\vec{W}) \Rightarrow \frac{1}{2}(20)V_A^2 - 5760 = mgh \Rightarrow 10 V_A^2 - 5760 = 20(10)(36) \Rightarrow 10 V_A^2 = 12960 \Rightarrow V_A = \sqrt{1296} = $ 36 m/s.

    2. $M.E_B = K.E_B + P.E_B = \frac{1}{2}(60)(\sqrt{70})^2 + 0 = 30(70) = 2100 \text{ J}$. Since $M.E_B = M.E_A$, mechanical energy is conserved, proving AB is frictionless.
