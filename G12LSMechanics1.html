Chapter XI: WORK AND ENERGY

Use: $g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$
Exercise 1
During a siege, a catapult throws a stone (R) considered as a particle, of mass $m = 15 \text{ kg}$ from a point O, $45 \text{ m}$ above the surface of the ground. The stone is thrown with an initial velocity $\vec{V}_0$ making an angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal and having a magnitude of $V_0 = 20 \text{ m/s}$.

S is the highest point reached by the stone, and it is at an altitude of $H = 6.4 \text{ m}$ above point O. Take the horizontal plane containing O as a reference level for gravitational potential energy (GPE = 0) and neglect air resistance.
  1. The mechanical energy of the system [(R) - Earth] is conserved. Why? Calculate its value.
    1. Calculate the gravitational potential energy of the system [(R) - Earth] at point S.
    2. Apply the principle of the conservation of mechanical energy to determine the speed of the stone $V_S$ at the highest point S.
    3. Knowing that $V_S = V_0 \cos(\alpha)$, deduce the value of $\alpha$.
  3. Apply the work-energy theorem to determine the speed $V_A$ of the stone as it reaches the ground.

In this problem, we study the ascending motion of the object.
A) Motion without air resistance
The graph of figure (1) shows the mechanical energy of the system [Object - Earth] as a function of $z$, the altitude of (G) relative to the reference level.
  1. Use figure (1) to:
    1. Show that the object is not subjected to air resistance during its ascending;

  2. Write the expression of the gravitational potential energy of the system [Object - Earth] as a function of $z$, and then draw on figure (1) its curve.
  3. Draw, on figure (1), the curve that represents the kinetic energy of the object as a function of $z$.

Exercise 3
Consider a skateboarder taken as a particle moving along a path ABCD in the vertical plane. The mass of the skateboarder and his equipment is $M = 50 \text{ kg}$. Along the track BC, whose length is $5 \text{ m}$, the skateboarder is acted upon by a constant friction force of magnitude $f = 40 \text{ N}$. The tracks AB and CD are frictionless.

The horizontal plane containing BC is taken as a reference level for gravitational potential energy.

The skateboarder starts from rest from point A at an altitude $h_A = 4.5 \text{ m}$ above the track BC.
  1. Along the track AB
    1. Determine the mechanical energy of the system Skateboarder-Earth when the skateboarder is at point A.
    2. The skateboarder reaches point B with a speed $V_B = \sqrt{90} \text{ m/s}$. Prove that the track AB is frictionless.
  2. Along the track BC
    1. Determine the work done by each of the external forces acting on the skateboarder between B and C.
    2. Deduce the speed of the skateboarder at C.
    3. The kinetic energy lost between B and C is converted into another form of energy. Name it.
  3. Along the track CD
    1. Calculate:
      1. the mechanical energy of the system Skateboarder-Earth when the skateboarder is at point C.
      2. the gravitational potential energy of the system Skateboarder-Earth when the skateboarder is at point D.
    2. Deduce that the skateboarder cannot reach point D.
    3. In reality, I is the highest point the skateboarder can reach before returning back. Determine the height $h_I$ of point I above the track BC.
Exercise 4
An object (S), taken as a particle of mass $m = 1.5 \text{ kg}$, is released from rest at a point A of the track ABC.
Given: $\sin(\alpha) = 0.25$; $\cos(\alpha) = 0.97$; $AB = 5 \text{ m}$ and BC is frictionless.
The horizontal plane containing BC is taken as a reference level for gravitational potential energy.
    1. (S) reaches a point B with a speed $V_B = 4 \text{ m/s}$. Determine the magnitude of the constant friction force exerted by the track AB on (S).
    2. Determine the instantaneous power of: the friction force, normal force, and the weight of (S), when the speed of (S) becomes $2 \text{ m/s}$.
    3. It takes the object $2.5 \text{ s}$ to move from A to B. Deduce the average power of the friction force acting on (S) between A and B.
  1. The object (S) continues its motion and reaches a point C, where it collides with another object (P) of mass $M = 2.5 \text{ kg}$, attached to one end of an inextensible light vertical cord of length $l = 80 \text{ cm}$. The other end of the cord is attached to a fixed point O.
    Right after collision between (S) and (P), object (P) is ejected with a speed of $V_0 = 1.5 \text{ m/s}$. When (P) reaches point D, the cord is deviated by a maximum angle $\beta$. Determine the value of $\beta$.
  2. As object (P) reaches point D, the cord is cut and (P) falls vertically downwards.
    1. Determine the mechanical energy of the system [(P) - Earth] at point D.
    2. Determine the speed of (P) and its altitude above ground when its kinetic energy becomes equal to the gravitational potential energy of the system [(P) - Earth].
    3. Determine the speed $V_{ground}$ of (P) upon reaching the ground.
