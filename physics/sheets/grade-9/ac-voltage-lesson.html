The 4 characteristics of an alternating sinusoidal voltage

Sv = 5 V/div · Sh = 5 ms/div

The oscilloscope has two settings: the vertical sensitivity Sv (V/div) and the horizontal sensitivity (time base) Sh (ms/div). Count y from the zero line to a peak, and x for one complete pattern.

Worked example (figure): y=3 div, so Um=5×3=15 V; x=4 div, so T=5×4=20 ms=0.02 s; f=10.02=50 Hz; Ueff=152≈10.6 V.

1. Maximum voltage

Um=Sv×y
the highest value of the voltage, in volts (V).

2. Period

T=Sh×x
the time of one complete oscillation, in seconds (s).

3. Frequency

f=1T
the number of periods in one second, in hertz (Hz), with T in s.

4. Effective voltage

Ueff=Um2
shown by a voltmeter in AC mode (~), in volts (V).

Maximum voltage Um

reached only at some instants (the peaks); read on the oscilloscope. Between two peaks, the voltage is smaller.

Effective voltage Ueff

the value of a DC voltage that gives the same effect (same brightness of a lamp, same heating). It is the value written on devices and read by a voltmeter in AC mode. Always Ueff<Um.

Change Sv and Sh: the picture changes, but the voltage does not. Try Um = 12 V with Sv = 2 V/div: what happens?
