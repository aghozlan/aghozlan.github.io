Grouping of Lamps: Current and Voltage

Name: Class: Date:
Series (one single path)Parallel (between the same two junctions)
Current Same current everywhere: I=I1=I2=I3 Law of junctions: the current entering a junction equals the current leaving it, so I=I1+I2+I3
Voltage Law of addition: UPN=U1+U2+U3 Law of uniqueness: UPN=U1=U2=U3
n identical lamps Same voltage for each: U1=UPNn Same current for each: I1=In
Switch K Closed: it acts like a wire, so UK=0. Open: no current passes in its path (I=0), and UK equals the whole voltage of its path: UK=UPN in a series circuit, and UK=UAB when K is in a branch between junctions A and B.
Way of thinking. (1) Find the junctions, the points where three or more wires meet. (2) Components between two junctions with no junction between them are in series: they carry the same current. (3) Paths between the same two junctions are in parallel: they have the same voltage, and their currents add. (4) Go from P to N along any path: the voltages always add up to UPN. The voltage across a connecting wire is zero.
