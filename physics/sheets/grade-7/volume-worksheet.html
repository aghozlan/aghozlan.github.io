What is volume?

The volume of a body is the space it occupies. Its SI unit is the cubic meter (m³). Smaller units: dm³, cm³, mm³. For liquids we also use the liter (L) and the milliliter (mL).

1 L = 1 dm³ · 1 mL = 1 cm³
1 m³ = 1000 L

Volume of a liquid: the graduated cylinder

  1. Put the cylinder on a horizontal table.
  2. Find the graduation value (the volume of one small division): graduation value=difference betweentwo numbered marksnumber of divisions
  3. Place your eye at the level of the liquid surface.
  4. Read at the bottom of the curved surface (the meniscus).
Worked example. Between 60 and 80 there are 4 divisions: 80−604=5 mL. The bottom of the meniscus is 1 division above 60: V=60+5=65 mL=65 cm3.

Volume of an irregular solid: the displacement method

  1. Pour water into the cylinder and read V₁.
  2. Lower the solid gently on a thread until it is completely under water, then read the new level V₂. The water rises because the solid takes its place: Vsolid=V2−V1.
Worked example. V1=54 mL and V2=72 mL, so Vstone=V2−V1
Vstone=72−54=18 mL
Vstone=18 cm3

Too big for the cylinder? Use an overflow can filled up to the spout. The water pushed out is collected and measured: it has the same volume as the solid. Here: 45 mL, so 45 cm³.

Eureka! Archimedes discovered this idea in his bath, more than 2000 years ago.

Volume of a regular solid: use a formula (π≈3.14, same unit for all lengths)

Cube

V=a3
A dice with a=2 cm:
V=23
V=8 cm3

Rectangular prism

V=L×w×h
Our classroom: 8 m × 6 m × 3 m
V=8×6×3
V=144 m3 of air!

Cylinder

V=πr2h
Water tank: r=5 dm, h=13 dm
V=3.14×52×13
V≈1020 dm3 (L)

Sphere

V=43πr3
Football: r=11 cm
V=43×3.14×113
