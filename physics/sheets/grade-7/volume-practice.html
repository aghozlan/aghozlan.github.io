MISSION 3

The nurse's choice

★★ think

A nurse must give a child 18 mL of medicine. She has three graduated cylinders.

  1. Which cylinder can not be used? Why?
  2. B and C can both hold 18 mL. Which one gives the more precise measure? Why?
  3. On cylinder B, how many small divisions are there between 15 mL and 18 mL?

MISSION 5

The clever thirsty crow

★★★ story

In an old story, a thirsty crow finds a jug with a little water. Its beak cannot reach the water. So the crow drops stones into the jug, one by one! Each stone has a volume of 25 cm³.

  1. Read the volume of water in the jug.
  2. The beak reaches the 400 mL mark. How many stones must the crow drop?
  3. The crow drops 6 stones. What will the new reading be?
  4. Would the trick work with sugar cubes? With pieces of cork that float? Explain.
MISSION 6

The egg mystery

★★★ think

Hiba fills an overflow can up to the spout, then puts an egg in it. She collects 58 mL of water.

  1. What is the volume of the egg in cm³?
  2. Why didn't she use a graduated cylinder directly?
  3. Her brother repeats it but forgets to fill the can up to the spout. He finds 40 mL. Is his answer too big or too small? Why?
MISSION 7

Volumes all around us

★★★ formulas

A. The aquarium.

  1. Calculate the volume of the full aquarium in cm³, then in L.
  2. We fill it only up to 35 cm. How many liters of water do we need?
  3. How many bottles of 1.5 L is that?

B. The box of dice. How many dice of side 2 cm fill this box exactly? Find the answer in two ways: with volumes, and by counting the dice along each edge.

C. Is the label honest? The can has radius 3.3 cm and height 11.5 cm.

  1. Calculate the volume of the can.
  2. The label says 330 mL. Why is your answer bigger?

D. Big or many? Which holds more air: one basketball or eight tennis balls? Calculate, then decide.

Home Lab: Make your own graduated cylinder!

Goal: build a graduated bottle, measure the volume of a small solid, and film a short video (1 to 3 minutes).

You need

a clear bottle or tall glass (an adult cuts the top) · a measuring cup, medicine cup or syringe · water, tape, a marker, a thread · a small solid that sinks and does not dissolve (stone, key, toy car, egg) · a phone.

Steps

  1. Stick a strip of tape from the bottom to the top of the bottle.
  2. Pour 50 mL of water at a time. After each pour, put your eye at the water level and mark it: 50, 100, 150…
  3. Empty some water, then read the level: this is V₁.
  4. Tie the solid to the thread and lower it gently until it is completely under water. Read V₂.
  5. Calculate V=V2−V1 and write it on a paper. Try a second object too!

In your video, show: your materials · how you marked the bottle · V₁ and V₂ in close-up, with the phone at the water level · your calculation on paper. Say your first name and class. You may film only your hands.
