Mass and Density: Heavy, Light… or Dense?

Name: Class: Date:
Objectives: define mass and give its SI unit · measure the mass of a solid and of a liquid with a balance · calculate the density ρ=m/V and convert its units · identify a substance by its density · predict if a body floats or sinks.

Mass m

The quantity of matter in a body. It does not change if we move the body (Beirut, Faraya or the Moon!).

SI unit: the kilogram (kg).
1 t = 1000 kg · 1 kg = 1000 g · 1 g = 1000 mg

Measured with a balance: two-pan balance (masses on the other pan) or electronic scale.

Mass of a liquid: mliquid=m2−m1, where m1 = empty container and m2 = container + liquid.

Tare (T): put the empty container, press T (the display shows 0), then pour: the scale shows the liquid only.

Density ρ (rho)

ρ=mV

The mass of one unit of volume. Units: g/cm³ (or g/mL) and kg/m³ (SI).

1 g/cm3=1000 kg/m3

Also: m=ρ×V and V=mρ.

Float or sink? A body put in a liquid floats if ρbody<ρliquid, and sinks if ρbody>ρliquid.
Worked example. A stone has m=52 g and V=20 cm3 (measured by displacement). ρ=mV=5220=2.6 g/cm3=2600 kg/m3. Since 2.6>1, it sinks in water.

Home challenge (bonus): build a rainbow density tower!

You need: a tall clear glass, honey, water with a drop of food coloring, olive oil, a kitchen measuring cup, and small objects (a grape, a cork, a small plastic toy, a coin).

  1. Pour 50 mL of honey slowly into the glass.
  2. Pour 50 mL of colored water slowly along the wall of the glass (or over the back of a spoon).
  3. Pour 50 mL of olive oil the same way. Watch the three layers form.
  4. Before dropping each object, predict where it will stop. Then drop it gently. Were you right?
  5. Extra: put the measuring cup on a kitchen scale, use the tare, and measure the mass of 50 mL of each liquid. Calculate each density.
